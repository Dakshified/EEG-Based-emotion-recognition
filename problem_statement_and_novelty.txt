================================================================================
PROJECT: EEG-Based Emotion Recognition (SEED-IV)
Frequency-Aware, Explainable, Responsible AI Approach
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROBLEM STATEMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Emotion detection from EEG signals: given a person's multi-channel EEG 
recording, classify their emotional state into one of four categories 
(neutral, sad, fear, happy), using the SEED-IV dataset.

This is a well-established problem in affective computing. The contribution 
of this work does not lie in the problem itself, but in HOW it is solved: 
with a rigorously evaluated, explainable, and trustworthy architecture that 
goes beyond raw accuracy as the sole measure of success.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DATASET
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SEED-IV (SJTU Emotion EEG Dataset - IV)
- 15 subjects, 3 sessions each, 24 trials per session
- 62-channel EEG (10-20 system)
- 4 emotion classes: Neutral, Sad, Fear, Happy
- Differential Entropy (DE) features with LDS smoothing, 5 frequency bands
  (Delta 1-4Hz, Theta 4-8Hz, Alpha 8-14Hz, Beta 14-31Hz, Gamma 31-50Hz)
- Final processed dataset: 37,575 samples (verified, leakage-free)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CORE NOVELTY CONTRIBUTIONS (10)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. GRANGER-CAUSALITY-INFORMED GAT ADJACENCY
   Claim: A graph built from directional, predictive relationships between 
   EEG channels (Granger causality) produces a more stable, less 
   over-smoothed structural prior than a fully-learned dense adjacency.
   Method: Compare DGCNN's fully-learned graph, a distance-based k-NN graph, 
   and a Granger-causality-informed graph, all feeding the same GAT.
   Evidence: DGCNN's learned adjacency measurably collapsed (avg degree 
   12.39 -> 1.35, sparsity -> 97.78%); test whether a causally-informed 
   graph avoids this failure mode.

2. FAITHFULNESS-VERIFIED EXPLAINABILITY
   Claim: Attention maps alone do not prove a model's explanation is real; 
   faithfulness must be quantitatively tested.
   Method: Deletion/insertion AUC testing on attention-based band/channel 
   importance rankings, compared against random-removal as a control.
   Evidence: Deletion/insertion AUC curves showing explanations are 
   meaningfully better than random.

3. CALIBRATED UNCERTAINTY WITH ABSTENTION
   Claim: A trustworthy EEG emotion model should know when it is uncertain 
   and be allowed to abstain rather than always forcing a guess.
   Method: Evidential/MC-Dropout uncertainty head; Expected Calibration 
   Error (ECE) and accuracy-vs-rejection curve.
   Evidence: ECE score, reliability diagram, accuracy improvement when 
   low-confidence predictions are excluded.

4. NOISE/ARTIFACT-ROBUST TRAINING (SIMULATED ELECTRODE FAILURE)
   Claim: A model explicitly trained to tolerate missing EEG channels 
   remains more reliable under real-world sensor conditions.
   Method: Train a variant with random channel-masking augmentation; 
   evaluate all models on the same corrupted test set (channels 
   progressively dropped).
   Evidence: Accuracy-vs-corruption-severity degradation curves across 
   all models.

5. EXPLANATION STABILITY UNDER NOISE (NEW METRIC)
   Claim: A trustworthy model's reasoning should stay consistent under 
   small input perturbations, not just its final answer.
   Method: Compare attention maps on clean vs. slightly-noised input 
   (cases where prediction stayed correct) via cosine similarity/rank 
   correlation.
   Evidence: A stability score - a new evaluation axis not currently 
   reported in EEG-emotion literature.

6. FAITHFULNESS-VS-CAPACITY TRADE-OFF CURVE
   Claim: In small-per-subject-data regimes, explanation faithfulness may 
   degrade BEFORE accuracy does, as model size increases.
   Method: Train 3-4 size variants of the proposed model; plot both 
   accuracy and faithfulness against parameter count.
   Evidence: A transferable, citable insight on the relationship between 
   model capacity and explanation reliability.

7. PER-BAND AUXILIARY SUPERVISION (WEIGHTED MULTI-LOSS)
   Claim: Supervising each frequency band's own mini-classifier (not just 
   the final fused prediction) improves both accuracy and interpretability 
   of which bands matter.
   Method: Auxiliary loss per band, dynamically weighted by that band's 
   own current accuracy.
   Evidence: Ablation (with vs. without auxiliary loss) - accuracy delta 
   + per-band importance ranking as a free explainability output.

8. NEUROSCIENCE-GROUNDED VALIDATION OF LEARNED ATTENTION
   Claim: The model's learned spatial/spectral attention should align with 
   established EEG-emotion physiology (e.g., frontal alpha asymmetry, 
   gamma-band arousal engagement).
   Method: Statistically compare attention-highlighted channels/bands 
   against literature-reported regions per emotion class.
   Evidence: A concrete interpretation table (band x region x attention 
   sign x known finding).

9. SUBJECT-LEVEL FAIRNESS AND RELIABILITY PROFILING
   Claim: Reporting only mean accuracy hides which individuals a model 
   actually fails for.
   Method: Per-subject accuracy, worst-case subject performance, and 
   uncertainty decomposition (aleatoric vs. epistemic) to check whether 
   failure is due to noisy signal or the model under-learning that subject.
   Evidence: Per-subject reliability table flagging any subject the model 
   is silently unreliable for.

10. CROSS-ARCHITECTURE ROBUSTNESS BENCHMARKING UNDER IDENTICAL NOISE
    Claim: No existing SEED-IV study benchmarks classical ML, sequence 
    models, graph models, and transformers all under the exact same 
    simulated electrode-failure conditions.
    Method: Apply the same corrupted-test protocol to SVM, CNN-LSTM, 
    DGCNN, Transformer, and both GAT-KAN variants - one unified 
    robustness comparison.
    Evidence: A single degradation-curve figure comparing 6 architecture 
    families - a reusable reference for future EEG robustness research.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ONE-PARAGRAPH SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Using EEG-based emotion recognition on SEED-IV as the testbed, this work 
proposes a Granger-causality-informed Graph Attention Network combined with 
cross-band Transformer attention and a parameter-efficient KAN classification 
head. Beyond accuracy, the model's explanations are verified for faithfulness 
and stability, its confidence is calibrated with an abstention mechanism, its 
performance is reported fairly across individual subjects, and its resilience 
to simulated sensor/electrode failure is benchmarked against four classical 
and deep learning baselines under an identical, rigorously leakage-free 
evaluation protocol.
